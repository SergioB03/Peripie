# Building a shop you can walk into without asking

I run a painting company. My customers call to ask one thing: do you have it? I don't want them to wait on hold or drive by if the paint isn't there. The virtual-store platforms that exist sell to large brands with huge catalogs. Nobody serves small local shops like mine. Some days I am also the customer who won't ask. I will order at a drive-through before I will ask at a counter, and a website cannot answer the question anyway. Peripie is a counter you can walk up to from wherever you are. You watch the clerk turn to the stock room before answering, so the answer is one you can trust.

A visit starts on the street. The storefront faces the road. You tap Enter shop or the doors open. The camera walks you in and the clerk says hello out loud. You ask for a product by typing or by voice. The clerk turns to the stock agent. The label reads Checking with the stock room. You hear the answer: in stock and how many, or out and when it is back. Under the clerk's words sits the stock room's own record. A bakery counts loaves and talks about trays and the oven. A paint store talks about cans and the supplier's truck. Each shop speaks in its own words because each time the clerk gives is that shop's real schedule.

The first technical decision was forcing the tool call on the first Gemini round. The prompt says always check stock but a prompt is a suggestion. Function-calling mode ANY on the first round is a rule. Without it the model can answer yes we have that from memory and the answer never touches the database. This ensures correct answers and stops hallucinations.

The second decision was using a time-series database to measure restock times. The stock room is a Postgres table with sixty days of shelf counts per product. A LAG window over those counts finds each spell at zero. Usually about four days is measured from history, not guessed. Node turns the date into Friday October 16 so the model never does calendar math. A bakery does not restock in three days it bakes. A schedule per item made the answers sound like the shop.

The third decision was composing the answer when the model fails. Live runs showed model timeouts and rate limits. The server reads the stock record and composes the reply from the date count and typical days. It returns 200 with audio. A bad minute for the model still gives a correct spoken answer. This fallback is what lets you demo at all at a venue.

Building for people the usual channels leave out was part of the design. The accessibility drawer offers captions larger text high contrast and reduce motion seeded from your phone's own settings and kept for this visit only. Describe this view reads out what is on screen in the browser's own voice without touching the network. The mic asks for permission once. On an iPhone the words may appear only when you stop talking. Replay plays the greeting or the last answer again. It works from memory so nothing is stored. Preferences stay in memory because saving them would break Zero Data.

The weekend had its problems. On the first real run gemini-3.8-flash answered 503 to every call then 429 and a hang ate the whole 10 s budget. The fix was gemini-3.5-flash-lite first about a second per answer 3.8-flash second and a 5 s cap on each attempt. A 12 second page timeout against a 12 second server deadline was a tie a slow real answer would lose. The budget is now 15 s for the page 10 s for the model and the voice gets the time left before 12.5 s. At 3 am Replit's Agent rewrote the workspace and a republish rolled the site back. The fix was a clean re-import of main into a new Repl.

The sandbox builder is what is next. Owners assemble their own shop from ready-made elements and staff it with agents. A free shop with one clerk and paid add-on agents. Each shop is already one settings file. The builder lets an owner edit it by dragging things around. After that comes a photo of the real storefront for the sign and walls checkout and the clerk running on the shop's own hardware.
